01

Why FSA-Bench?

Clinical time-to-event data are routinely collected across multiple healthcare institutions but are rarely pooled because of privacy constraints. Federated Survival Analysis enables collaborative modeling without sharing patient-level data, but model reliability, calibration, robustness, and explanation consistency remain insufficiently understood.

Benchmark goal

Evaluate statistical, machine learning, and deep learning survival models under heterogeneous, non-IID, multi-institutional federated settings.

05

Key findings

01

Federated ≈ centralized discrimination

Federated models achieve discrimination comparable to centralized approaches across many datasets, particularly for larger cohorts such as TCGA, LeukSurv, SEER, and Dialysis.

02

Censoring matters

Predictive performance generally declines as censoring increases, with a more pronounced decline beyond approximately 50% censoring.

03

Deep federated models remain competitive

FedPDNN, FedPLSTM, and FedPAttn maintain competitive discrimination and calibration across heterogeneous and highly censored settings.

04

No single model wins everything

Predictive performance and explanation stability provide complementary evidence. Fed CoxPH is especially strong in ranking consistency, while FedPLSTM and FedPAttn provide stable explanations among deep models.

06

Explanation stability

FSA-Bench extends evaluation beyond predictive accuracy using SurvSHAP and rank-based agreement metrics. Feature-importance consistency is evaluated at the fold, site, and centralized-versus-federated partition levels.

Fold-levelConsistency across cross-validation folds
Site-levelConsistency across heterogeneous clients
Partition-levelCentralized vs. federated explanations
07

Statistical comparison

Pairwise two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests compare FedPAttn with eight federated survival baselines. Holm correction is applied across the eight comparisons.

Fed. KM10–0 wins · pHolm = 0.0156
DeepPseudo9–1 wins · pHolm = 0.0410
DeepHit7–1 wins · pHolm = 0.1406
LinearPH6–2 wins · pHolm = 0.2734

Statistical significance is evaluated using dataset-level mean C-index values as paired experimental units; missing results are excluded from the corresponding comparison.
